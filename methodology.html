How worldbook.earth is built

Every arc traces to a named public dataset. Nothing here is drawn from intuition.

Worldbook is a layered flow-map of the world. Each layer is a different kind of global flow — remittances, trade, grain, oil, refugees, student migration, submarine cables, and the illicit side too: drugs, arms, wildlife, counterfeit goods. Every arc you see is drawn from a named, public dataset. If a flow appears on the map, a source reports it. If a number sets an arc's weight, that number comes from the citation attached to the layer.

This page exists so you never have to take that on faith. Everything below is checkable.

The one rule: Every number traces to a named source. Geometry is not fabricated. Absence is not evidence of absence.

What the arc weights mean — and what they don't

Not every layer measures the same kind of thing, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. There are three tiers, and worldbook labels each one in the app:

  1. Hard quantities. Remittances, debt, migration, and trade carry real figures — US$ billions, millions of people — from the bilateral matrices that report them. An arc's thickness is proportional to that figure.
  2. Route prominence. Oil, grain, minerals, timber, flights, shipping, students, and refugees show the major documented corridors, weighted by relative scale. These are the principal flows, not an exhaustive census of every route.
  3. Relative, seizure-based signal. Human trafficking, wildlife trafficking, counterfeit goods, and medical brain drain encode relative corridor prominence — because the underlying data is detections, seizures, and reported cases, not a clean count of the true flow. An arc here means "this corridor shows up prominently in enforcement data," not "this many units moved."

Submarine cables are shown at uniform weight; for fibre routes, thickness carries no meaning.

The thing critics get wrong: absence is not evidence of absence

This is the single most important caveat on the entire map, and it applies hardest to the illicit layers.

A thick arms-trafficking arc into a country means the Small Arms Survey and UNODC documented flows there. A thin arc, or no arc, very often means no data was reported — not that no trafficking occurs. Enforcement data reflects where monitoring, seizures, and reporting happen, which is uneven across the world for reasons that have nothing to do with the true scale of the activity. Countries with strong reporting regimes can look "worse" precisely because they measure and disclose more.

So: do not read these maps as moral rankings of countries. They are maps of where data exists. That's a real and useful thing to see — but it is not the same as a map of where the underlying activity happens, and worldbook does not claim it is.

Territorial units

Borders and territorial units follow the reporting conventions of whichever source supplies a given layer's data. Taiwan, Hong Kong, Kosovo, Western Sahara, and other contested or special cases appear as their cited sources report them — sometimes as distinct reporting economies, sometimes folded into a larger state, depending on the dataset.

This reflects data provenance. It is not a sovereignty claim, and it is not an editorial position on any territorial dispute. Where a source reports a unit separately, worldbook keeps it separate rather than silently overriding the source; where a source consolidates, worldbook follows.

What this is, and what it isn't

Worldbook is research-grade and approximate. Figures are rounded and, on some layers, blended across the sources named above. It's built for exploration — to make a set of otherwise opaque global systems navigable in one view — not as an authoritative reference you'd cite in a paper. For that, go to the primary sources. They're all linked, on purpose.

Found something wrong?

Good — that's the point. If a corridor looks incorrect, a source is misread, or a flow is missing that the data would support, that's exactly the kind of correction that makes the map better. The whole thing is built to be checkable so that errors are findable. Bring the source and it gets fixed.
